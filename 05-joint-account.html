Chapter 5

The Joint Account With the Girlfriend

Why "joint" doesn't mean "one of us can file for both of us" when you're not married

Two years in, things got serious, and Hipster and his girlfriend opened a joint local account for rent, groceries, and the occasional trip. Both names, either can withdraw everything. Practically speaking, it's shared.

Hipster figured "joint" meant one FBAR could cover them both, the way he'd heard married couples do it. Close, but no.

The real shortcut is a spousal exception, not a joint-account exception. Under the FBAR instructions (Part III), a non-filing spouse can skip their own FBAR only if all the accounts they'd report are jointly owned with the filing spouse, the filing spouse timely e-files an FBAR reporting them, and both spouses sign FinCEN Form 114a (Record of Authorization to Electronically File FBARs). The couple keeps Form 114a; it goes to FinCEN or the IRS only on request.

Every condition assumes a marriage. His girlfriend isn't his spouse, so the exception doesn't apply, however long they've dated. Each of them has a financial interest in the account under 31 CFR § 1010.350(e)(1), and each reports its entire value, not half.

So Hipster reports the joint account at full value on his own FBAR, next to his personal and company accounts. If she's a US person, she reports it on hers too. If she isn't, she has no FBAR duty on it, but her status doesn't shrink his.

The Law, Actually

  • FinCEN Form 114 Instructions, Part III; FinCEN guidance on the filing spouse (fincen.gov/filing-spouse) -- the spousal FBAR filing exception applies only when accounts are jointly owned between spouses, the filing spouse timely e-files, and both spouses execute FinCEN Form 114a. It does not extend to unmarried joint owners.
  • 31 CFR § 1010.350(e)(1) -- when an account is maintained in the names of more than one person, each US person named has a financial interest in it.
  • FinCEN FBAR Electronic Filing Instructions -- "Each joint owner must report the entire value of the account."
  • FinCEN Form 114a -- the authorization record used for the spousal exception; retained by the filer, not submitted with the FBAR, but producible on request.

Bottom Line

Only spouses get the joint-filing shortcut, and only when every condition is met. An unmarried joint owner who is a US person reports the account at full value on their own FBAR, regardless of what the other owner does.
