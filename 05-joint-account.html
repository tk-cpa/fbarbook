Chapter 5

The Joint Account With the Girlfriend

Why "joint" doesn't mean "one of us can file for both of us" when you're not married

[ILLUSTRATION -- Hipster and his girlfriend splitting a rent receipt at a kitchen table, a joint bank card between them]

Two years in, Hipster's relationship got serious enough that he and his girlfriend opened a joint local account to split rent, groceries, and the occasional trip. Both names are on it. Either of them can withdraw the full balance. It functions, in every practical sense, like a shared account.

Hipster assumed that if it's joint, one FBAR ought to cover it, the way he'd heard married couples sometimes handle theirs. That assumption is where he went wrong, and it's worth being precise about why.

There is a real FBAR shortcut for joint accounts, but it's a spousal exception, not a joint-account exception. Under 31 CFR § 1010.350(g), a non-filing spouse can be excused from filing their own FBAR, but only when all the accounts that spouse would otherwise have to report are jointly owned with the filing spouse, the filing spouse reports those accounts on a timely, electronically filed FBAR, and both spouses complete and sign FinCEN Form 114a (Record of Authorization to Electronically File FBARs) -- the filing spouse completing Part II, both completing Part I. That form isn't filed with the FBAR; it's retained by the couple and produced to FinCEN or the IRS only if requested.

Every condition in that exception is built around a marriage. Hipster's girlfriend is not his spouse. There is no version of 31 CFR § 1010.350(g) that extends to an unmarried partner, however long the relationship, however joint the account. Each of them, individually, has a financial interest in the full account under 31 CFR § 1010.350(b) -- joint ownership means each owner reports the account's full value, not half of it -- and each of them independently crosses into FBAR territory if their own aggregate foreign accounts, this one included, exceed $10,000 at any point in the year.

Practically: Hipster reports the joint account on his own FBAR, at its full value, alongside his personal and company accounts. His girlfriend, if she's also a US person, needs to do the same on her own FBAR. If she isn't a US person at all, the account isn't reportable on her end in the first place, but it's still fully reportable on his -- her non-US status doesn't shrink his own obligation.

The Law, Actually

  • 31 CFR § 1010.350(g) -- the spousal FBAR filing exception applies only when accounts are jointly owned between spouses, the filing spouse timely e-files, and both spouses execute FinCEN Form 114a. It does not extend to unmarried joint owners.
  • 31 CFR § 1010.350(b) -- each joint owner of an account has a financial interest in the account's full value, not a proportionate share.
  • FinCEN Form 114a -- the authorization record used for the spousal exception; retained by the filer, not submitted with the FBAR, but producible on request.

Bottom Line

Only spouses get the joint-filing shortcut, and only when every condition in 31 CFR 1010.350(g) is met. An unmarried joint account holder who is a US person reports that account on their own FBAR at full value, full stop, regardless of what the other joint owner does.
