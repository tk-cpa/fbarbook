Chapter 8

What's Next

Hipster's grandmother, a gift from abroad, and the book that comes after this one

[ILLUSTRATION -- Hipster on a video call with his grandmother, who is holding up a photo and smiling]

With the FBAR filed, Hipster figured the paperwork chapter of his life abroad was closed. It wasn't. His grandmother, still living in the country he grew up visiting, has spent years quietly mentioning that she wants to help him with a down payment someday. She means it as a gift, plainly, and there's nothing wrong with that. But the moment money actually moves from her account to his, a different reporting question opens up, one that has nothing to do with the accounts this book covered.

The FBAR only ever asked one thing: where is money sitting. It didn't care where that money came from, who sent it, or why. A gift or inheritance from a foreign person is a different category entirely, with its own threshold, its own form, and its own penalty structure, unconnected to anything in this book except the same instinct that got Hipster this far: don't assume, check the actual rule, and file the thing on time.

His girlfriend and his foreign co-founders aren't going anywhere either. The company that gave him signature authority in Chapter 3 has its own filing obligations once ownership crosses certain lines. None of that gets resolved here. It gets resolved in the next book.

Coming Next in the Series

Hipster's grandmother's gift is where the next book picks up: what counts as a reportable gift or bequest from someone abroad, the filing threshold, and Form 3520. The company from Chapter 3, and what his foreign co-founders mean for his own filing obligations, carries forward from there too.
