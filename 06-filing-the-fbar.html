Chapter 6

Actually Filing the Thing

FinCEN Form 114, the BSA E-Filing System, deadlines, and the automatic extension nobody has to ask for

Once Hipster accepted he had three accounts to report (personal, company, joint), the mechanics were less dramatic than getting there. The FBAR isn't attached to a tax return and isn't filed through IRS e-file. It goes straight to FinCEN through the BSA E-Filing System, a free government portal. He can file it himself, or a paid preparer can, with a signed Form 114a on record.

For each account, the form asks for the institution's name and address, the account number, the account type, and the maximum value during the year, plus extra owner details for joint and signature-authority-only accounts. Pulling statements for all three accounts took longer than the filing.

The deadline is April 15 of the following year, the same as the 1040, thanks to the Surface Transportation and Veterans Health Care Choice Improvement Act of 2015 (Pub. L. 114-41, § 2006(b)(11)). And the extension is a freebie: FinCEN grants an automatic extension to October 15 to anyone who misses April 15. No form, no letter, no reason. File by October 15 and you're not late.

One filing doesn't cover everything. The FBAR reports accounts and their values. It doesn't report the income those accounts earned (that belongs on the 1040), and it doesn't cover his ownership of a foreign company, which can trigger its own information return. Those questions belong to other chapters and other books.

The Law, Actually

  • Pub. L. 114-41, § 2006(b)(11) -- sets the FBAR due date at April 15, with a maximum extension to October 15, for reports for taxable years beginning after December 31, 2015.
  • FinCEN FBAR Electronic Filing Instructions -- FinCEN grants an automatic extension to October 15 to filers who miss April 15, with no request required; a filer using a third-party preparer completes and keeps Form 114a and does not file it.
  • IRS, Comparison of Form 8938 and FBAR Requirements (page last reviewed September 19, 2026) -- the FBAR is filed electronically through FinCEN's BSA E-Filing System and is not filed with a federal tax return; due April 15 with an automatic 6-month extension to October 15.

Bottom Line

File FinCEN Form 114 through the BSA E-Filing System, separately from your tax return. It's due April 15, with an automatic extension to October 15. Filing it on time doesn't mean your foreign income or foreign company ownership was reported correctly elsewhere.
