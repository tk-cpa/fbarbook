Chapter 3

The Company Account He Can Move Money From

Signature authority over a foreign company account he doesn't own a majority of -- and why control is a separate trigger from ownership

Hipster didn't start the company alone. He co-founded it with two local partners and holds 20 percent. The partners handle strategy; Hipster handles operations, which means he's the one who can log into the company's bank account and move money: pay a vendor, cover a subscription, run payroll. The account never held more than the equivalent of $30,000, and none of it ever felt like his.

That instinct is right about ownership and wrong about the FBAR. Ownership and control are separate triggers, and Hipster has only one of them.

Start with ownership. Under 31 CFR § 1010.350(e)(2), a US person has a financial interest in a foreign company's account only if he owns, directly or indirectly, more than 50 percent of the corporation's voting power or total value (or more than 50 percent of a partnership's profits or capital). At 20 percent, Hipster has no financial interest in the company's account through ownership.

Control is the second trigger, and it's the one that catches him. Signature or other authority, defined at 31 CFR § 1010.350(f)(1), is the authority of an individual, alone or together with another, to control the disposition of money or other assets in a financial account by direct communication with the bank. If Hipster can wire money out, he has it, whether or not a dollar of that money is his.

There is a narrow exemption in 31 CFR § 1010.350(f)(2) for certain officers and employees of banks and other specified regulated entities, when they have no financial interest in the account. It exists for people like a bank's own staff, not for the operations lead of a small startup. It doesn't help Hipster.

The Law, Actually

  • 31 CFR § 1010.350(f)(1) -- signature or other authority is the authority of an individual (alone or in conjunction with another) to control the disposition of money, funds or other assets in a financial account by direct communication.
  • 31 CFR § 1010.350(e)(2) -- financial interest through an entity requires direct or indirect ownership of more than 50 percent of a corporation's voting power or total value, or of a partnership's profits or capital.
  • 31 CFR § 1010.350(f)(2) -- a narrow exemption for officers and employees of certain regulated financial institutions with no financial interest in the account; it does not cover a co-founder of an ordinary operating company.

Bottom Line

Being able to move money out of an account is its own FBAR trigger, separate from owning any of it. Hipster owns 20 percent of the company and has no ownership-based interest in its account, but his signature authority is enough on its own. The account goes on his personal FBAR as a signature-authority-only account, and its value counts toward the $10,000 aggregate test alongside everything else he reports.
