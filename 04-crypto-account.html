Chapter 4

The Crypto Exchange Account

Where the law is genuinely unsettled, and why "probably fine" isn't a filing position

[ILLUSTRATION -- Hipster's phone showing a crypto exchange app balance, a small question mark hovering above it]

Hipster also has an account on a crypto exchange headquartered outside the US, holding nothing but digital assets. No dollars sit in it, no local currency, just crypto. When his friend told him about FBAR, this was the account he was most worried about and, it turns out, the one with the least settled answer.

As of this writing, FinCEN's own position is unusually direct on this exact point. In FinCEN Notice 2020-2, the agency stated plainly that "a foreign account holding virtual currency is not reportable on the FBAR (unless it is a reportable account under 31 CFR 1010.350 because it holds reportable assets besides virtual currency)." In the same notice, FinCEN said it intends to propose amending the regulations to add virtual currency as its own category of reportable account. That proposal, as of the most recent check on this chapter, has not been finalized into a rule.

So the current, literal answer for an account holding only crypto is that it falls outside the existing regulatory definition of a reportable account. But two things keep this from being a comfortable place to stop. First, "not reportable" today is an artifact of a 2020 notice about a rule FinCEN has already said it plans to change; there is no guarantee about when or how that changes, or whether any transition rule applies once it does. Second, and more immediately: the exception only covers an account holding nothing but virtual currency. The moment Hipster's exchange account also holds a cash balance, a stablecoin FinCEN or a court later treats differently, or any other reportable asset, the account stops being crypto-only and becomes reportable in full under the ordinary rule, the same as any other foreign financial account.

This is exactly the kind of fact pattern that shouldn't be resolved from a general-audience book. It depends on precisely what the account holds, whether the exchange itself has since been reclassified by FinCEN guidance, and whatever the current state of that pending rulemaking is on the date the FBAR is due.

The Law, Actually

  • FinCEN Notice 2020-2 -- a foreign account holding solely virtual currency is not currently reportable on the FBAR, unless it also holds reportable assets besides virtual currency, in which case the account is reportable in full.
  • FinCEN Notice 2020-2 -- FinCEN stated its intent to propose amending 31 CFR 1010.350 to add virtual currency as its own category of reportable account; no final rule had been issued as of this chapter's most recent check.

Flagged for Verification

This is a live regulatory area, not a settled one. Before relying on "crypto-only foreign accounts are exempt" for any actual filing decision, confirm the current status of FinCEN's proposed virtual currency rule as of the year being filed, and confirm exactly what the specific account holds (crypto-only versus mixed). Do not treat this chapter as the final word on a rule FinCEN itself has said it intends to change.

Bottom Line

Right now, an account holding only foreign crypto sits outside the FBAR's current definition of a reportable account, per FinCEN's own 2020 notice. A mixed account does not get that treatment. And this is the one chapter in the book where "check again before you file" is the actual advice, not a hedge.
