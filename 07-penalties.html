Chapter 7

What Happens If He Doesn't

Non-willful and willful penalties, reasonable cause, and the procedures that exist for people who are late but not crooked

The penalty section is what finally got Hipster to sit down and file. Read it carefully: the gap between "forgot" and "hid it on purpose" is enormous.

Non-willful (no intent): up to $10,000 per violation by statute, adjusted for inflation. Under 31 CFR § 1010.821 (effective January 17, 2025; the 2026 adjustment was cancelled), the maximum is $16,536. There's a built-in exception: no penalty if the violation was due to reasonable cause and the account balance was properly reported.

Willful (deliberately false, or deliberately not filed): a different league. 31 U.S.C. § 5321(a)(5)(C) sets the maximum at the greater of $100,000 (inflation-adjusted to $165,353; the 50 percent prong isn't adjusted) or 50 percent of the account balance at the time of the violation. On a big account, 50 percent dwarfs the flat amount, and the reasonable cause exception doesn't apply.

Criminal: 31 U.S.C. § 5322(a) allows a fine up to $250,000, up to five years in prison, or both. Under § 5322(b), violating another federal law, or a pattern of illegal activity over $100,000 in 12 months, raises it to $500,000, ten years, or both.

None of that is Hipster. He's late, not hiding anything, and just didn't know. The IRS's advice for someone like him: if the IRS hasn't contacted you and you're not under investigation, file late FBARs as soon as possible to keep potential penalties to a minimum, and give the reason in the BSA E-Filing System (a drop-down, or "other" with a written explanation). If unreported foreign income is also in play, the Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures cover returns and FBARs together for non-willful filers, with a reduced penalty or none depending on where you live.

The Law, Actually

  • 31 U.S.C. § 5321(a)(5)(B)(i) -- non-willful maximum of $10,000 per violation, inflation-adjusted to $16,536 under 31 CFR § 1010.821 (effective Jan. 17, 2025); OMB Memorandum M-26-11 cancelled the 2026 adjustment.
  • 31 U.S.C. § 5321(a)(5)(B)(ii) -- no penalty if the violation was due to reasonable cause and the balance in the account was properly reported.
  • 31 U.S.C. § 5321(a)(5)(C), (D) -- willful maximum of the greater of $100,000 (adjusted to $165,353; the 50 percent prong is not adjusted) or 50 percent of the account balance at the time of the violation; the reasonable cause exception does not apply.
  • 31 U.S.C. § 5322(a) -- willful violation: fine up to $250,000, imprisonment up to 5 years, or both.
  • 31 U.S.C. § 5322(b) -- willful violation while violating another federal law, or as part of a pattern of illegal activity over $100,000 in 12 months: fine up to $500,000, imprisonment up to 10 years, or both.
  • IRS FBAR page; FinCEN late-filing instructions (fincen.gov/filing-late); Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures -- late FBAR filers who have not been contacted by the IRS should file as soon as possible and give the reason in the BSA E-Filing System; the Streamlined procedures address returns and FBARs together for non-willful filers.

Bottom Line

Penalty amounts adjust with inflation, so treat these dollar figures as current as of October 2026. Willful versus non-willful is the line that matters. If you're simply late and have nothing to hide, filing late beats not filing.
