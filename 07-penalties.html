Chapter 7

What Happens If He Doesn't

Non-willful and willful penalties, reasonable cause, and the procedures that exist for people who are late but not crooked

The penalty section is what finally got Hipster to sit down and file. Read it carefully: the range between "forgot" and "hid it on purpose" is enormous.

A non-willful violation, missing the filing or getting it wrong without intent, carries a civil penalty of up to $10,000 per violation under the original statute, a figure adjusted annually for inflation under the Federal Civil Penalties Inflation Adjustment Act. Under 31 CFR § 1010.821 (effective January 17, 2025, and still current in October 2026), that adjusted maximum is $16,536 per violation. There is a real exception built into the statute itself: no penalty applies if the violation was due to reasonable cause and the account balance was otherwise properly reported on the taxpayer's income tax return.

A willful violation, filing that's deliberately false or a deliberate failure to file, is a different order of magnitude. Under 31 U.S.C. § 5321(a)(5)(C), the maximum civil penalty is the greater of $100,000 (inflation-adjusted to $165,353 per 31 CFR § 1010.821; the 50 percent prong is not adjusted) or 50 percent of the account balance at the time of the violation. On a large account, the 50 percent figure can dwarf the flat-dollar number, and the reasonable cause exception does not apply to willful violations. That is why willfulness is the line that matters most.

Beyond civil exposure, 31 U.S.C. § 5322 creates criminal penalties for willful violations: a fine of up to $250,000, imprisonment of up to five years, or both, under § 5322(a). If the violation occurs while breaking another federal law, or as part of a pattern of illegal activity involving more than $100,000 in a 12-month period, § 5322(b) raises that to a fine of up to $500,000, imprisonment of up to ten years, or both.

None of that is Hipster's situation. He's late, not hiding anything, with nothing more sinister than not knowing the rule existed. For someone in that position, the IRS says that if it hasn't contacted you and you're not under investigation, you should file the late FBARs as soon as possible to keep potential penalties to a minimum, and explain the reason for filing late in the BSA E-Filing System (a drop-down list, or "other" with a written explanation). For someone who also has unreported foreign income to fix, the Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures address both the returns and the FBARs together, with reduced or no penalty depending on residency.

The Law, Actually

  • 31 U.S.C. § 5321(a)(5)(B) -- non-willful civil penalty, statutory cap of $10,000 per violation, inflation-adjusted to $16,536 per violation under 31 CFR § 1010.821 (adjustment effective Jan. 17, 2025; no later adjustment as of October 2026).
  • 31 U.S.C. § 5321(a)(5)(B)(ii) -- reasonable cause exception: no penalty if the failure was due to reasonable cause and the account was properly reported on the income tax return.
  • 31 U.S.C. § 5321(a)(5)(C), (D) -- willful civil penalty, the greater of $100,000 (inflation-adjusted to $165,353 under 31 CFR § 1010.821) or 50 percent of the account balance at the time of the violation; the reasonable cause exception does not apply.
  • 31 U.S.C. § 5322(a) -- criminal penalty for willful violations: fine up to $250,000, imprisonment up to 5 years, or both.
  • 31 U.S.C. § 5322(b) -- enhanced criminal penalty when combined with another federal law violation or a pattern exceeding $100,000 in illegal activity within 12 months: fine up to $500,000, imprisonment up to 10 years, or both.
  • IRS FBAR page (late filing); FinCEN late-filing instructions (fincen.gov/filing-late); Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures -- IRS and FinCEN guidance for late filers: file as soon as possible and explain the reason; the Streamlined procedures cover returns and FBARs together for non-willful filers.

Bottom Line

Penalty amounts adjust for inflation, so treat the dollar figures above as current as of October 2026, not fixed forever. The line that matters most is willful versus non-willful. For someone who is simply late and has nothing to hide, filing late beats not filing at all.
